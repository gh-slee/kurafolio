# ENG-116: Pilot the spec-driven loop and decide whether to enforce the Build plan gate

## Goal

The spec workflow is only worth its cost if planned tickets come back right from the builder models. Run three real tickets through the full loop (spec, Build plan, build, independent review, merge) with the builders chosen in ENG-115, record the outcomes, and decide from the evidence whether `next` and `claim` should refuse a standard-tier ticket without a Build plan.

## Context

- Loop and outcome line: [spec-development.md](../docs/spec-development.md#the-ticket-is-the-spec).
- Candidates at the time of writing: ENG-107 (a small `standard` test fix), a standard page ticket whose contract has merged, and IMP-007N (small, fully specified frontier ticket). An `any`-tier ticket may replace one of them; it needs no Build plan.

## Requirements

- Three tickets of different tiers, each built by the ENG-115 builder for its tier in its own session; `standard` and `frontier` ones are planned first by a frontier-tier session.
- Each ticket follows the spec format and records the outcome line at merge.
- The decision on enforcement is the owner's, from the recorded outcomes.
- If enforcement is chosen, `next` and `claim` refuse a `standard` ticket without a Build plan, with tests, and the documents drop the "until the pilot" wording. CI then runs `tickets.py check` and `tickets.py spec <ID> --base origin/main` for each ticket a pull request changes, and fails when a build branch changes gated sections.
- Recurring gaps go into the Architect skill's planning guidance.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] AC-ENG-116-01: Three tickets have outcome lines in their Logs: implementing model, reviewing model, repair rounds and what the spec missed.
